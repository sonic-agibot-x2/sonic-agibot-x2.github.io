Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Reuse of a Frozen Humanoid Motion Planner

Sitarama Chekuri  ·  Patrick Rose  ·  Claude Fable 5
Ultimate Bots  ·  Anthropic (AI co-author)  ·  draft preprint, 2026
📄 Paper (draft PDF) 🤗 Models (SONIC-X2) 💻 Code (release pending) ↩ SONIC-X2 port (companion report)

A 2M-step motion planner trained entirely on the Unitree G1 drives the AgiBot X2 Ultra at deployment quality — with zero gradient steps on its weights. An analytic joint-space wrap plus an 83k-parameter per-frame residual head, selected purely by closed-loop rollouts, beats both planners natively trained for the X2.

~10,000 H100-hours (the source planner's training schedule) replaced by ~6 hours on one workstation GPU of adaptation. The central finding is methodological: offline fit and closed-loop viability diverge — every time an offline metric argued, it argued wrong.

Slow walk, 0.3 m/s — the demo gait

Source robot (left, native planner) vs target robot (right, wrapped planner + heads + walk-start templates), identical velocity commands. Kinematic reference playback, daemon-faithful 16-frame serving. Watch the first step: the source's native gait initiation surges — a trait of its training corpus — while the target's first stride matches its steady gait, courtesy of operator-recorded initiation templates injected into the frozen planner's clip library (Section 9 of the paper). Operator verdict after driving it in simulation: "ready to ship."

Run, 3.0 m/s command — stress case

Out of the deployable envelope — bounded by the source core's own corpus and the tracker's training coverage, not by the transfer (an A/B isolation in the paper shows the native source core fails the same speed asks alone). Stride style transfers faithfully.

Crouch walk — a capability neither native planner had

Inherited for free from the source clip library through the wrap; commanded depth saturates at the source corpus's floor.
